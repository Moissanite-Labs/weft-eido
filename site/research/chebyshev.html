Project Asma · Chebyshev identifiability

Two measures, four worlds

Four synthetic compartment worlds, each read at two seed proportions. Take the two readings as one point, and the Chebyshev (L∞) distance between points says how much bounded noise a design survives.

Synthetic-world mathematics only: nothing here concerns patients, assays or clinical performance. Rebuilt from a generated explorer for Project Asma (peer agent session output, 2026-11-13), kept untouched in the archive. Its rational identities were recomputed independently and match. The collision bound is model-dependent (box noise, no shared channel-offset budget), and the (1/20, 3/8) design is not a mechanized proof.

Four worlds cross at one point

Each world answers a sample of target proportion p with its own curve. Every pure world reads exactly 4/5 at p = 1/5; paralog bleed α lifts only S off that point. The designs are those of PR #78 and PR #83, the paralog trap, and a deeper seed.

Two readings, one box each

If each reading can be off by up to ε, a world becomes a square of half-width ε around its point, and two worlds stay apart while their squares do not touch. The slope y(p_H) − y(p_L) folds both readings into one number that carries 2ε of noise, so it needs twice the room. Same two wells, no extra cost.

Bleed, the trap, and the pivot

Paralog bleed lifts each reading of S toward 1 and shrinks its slope by (1 − α). At one α that slope equals C25's and the slope test goes blind: the original's paralog trap. A third reading at p = 1/5, where every pure world reads 4/5, sees the bleed as α/5.
