Substrate simulations

A cell that may only wander inside a ball, a genotype that unfolds into a matrix two thousand times its size, and a graft that cannot touch what its host keeps.

Rebuilt from the Substrate Simulation Suite for weft. Where a figure computes, it computes here, live. Experiment results are the original page's reported numbers and are labelled reported; anything drawn only for look is labelled illustrative. The original is kept, untouched, in the archive.

The statistical cell: a walk inside a ball

An 8 × 8 genotype whose top two rows are frozen. Each A/B step proposes a small random change to the six plastic rows and keeps it only if the whole matrix stays within Frobenius distance R of where it began. The core cannot move; the periphery walks out to the wall, then mostly bounces off it.

1.20

From weft #141 and #147. The gate is the original's rule, ported exactly, with a seeded source in place of Math.random. Its fit is a counter, not a measurement. The original also names parametric (μ, Σ) and causal SCM do(X) decoders and a KL bound, but its code runs only this matrix rule, and its pointer stimulus never reaches the genotype, so neither is drawn here.

Morphogenesis: 492 bytes unfold

A 492-byte packet of 24 combinator nodes (CON, DUP, FRC, SUP, VLV, ERA) reduces into a Plasm weight matrix of 64² to 512² floats. The square beside the matrix is the packet, drawn to scale. The lines are E11's returns as reported: the unfolded arm gains as the matrix grows, while the scrambled packet and direct evolution of the matrix fall away.

512²

From weft #153 and #155 (E11). Returns and the core drift of 0.0000 are reported: the original prints them from a fixed table. The wire sizes are computed. The unfolding pattern is illustrative, the original's closed-form stand-in rather than the packet's reduction; its reduction-step counter and node ring reduced nothing and are left out.

Plasmid graft: the null-space gate

An 8-node, 172-byte plasmid is spliced onto a channel-permuted sibling's auxiliary port through SUP(5). The gate lands its update as ΔW(I − QkeepQkeepᵀ), so the directions the host's own skill uses cannot see it, whatever the shift.

2.4×

From weft #157 and #159 (E12). Left is a seeded toy computed here to show the operator itself; its numbers are not E12's, and it does not model why the ungated graft's transfer fails. Right is reported: the original gives each arm's retention at 1.0× and 2.4× shift; between them the line is its own linear interpolation, and beyond 2.4× (dashed) its extrapolation. Its drop and transfer labels do not move with shift.
