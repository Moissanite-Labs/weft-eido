Causal Harmonic Computing

The thesis is to compute by resonance. Each mechanism in the system is mapped to a chord built from whole-number frequency ratios. Coupled oscillators lock to those ratios, so you can hear whether the machine is settled. The numbers in the paper and pages are the authors' claims, not results this repository has checked.

What it can do

  • Play named states such as homeostasis and tampering as chords, in the browser.
  • Show a dissonance curve: how rough two tones sound as their ratio changes.
  • Show a group of oscillators falling into step when their coupling is strong enough.
  • Lay the whole idea out as a poster, and as a paper with a reference program.

Concepts

Mechanisms as chords

Six parts of the system each get a chord of simple ratios such as 4:5:6:7. Simple ratios sound consonant.

Roughness as tension

Two tones close in pitch beat against each other. A standard formula for that roughness is used as a measure of strain.

Locking

Coupled oscillators pull each other into step. The Kuramoto model describes when that happens. Below a critical coupling they drift. Above it they lock.

Causal means no looking ahead

Every stage uses only past and present input, at three time scales: rule systems, coupled oscillators, and sample-by-sample filters.

A trace of history

Moving between chords in pure tuning leaves a small pitch offset of 21.51 cents. The paper treats that leftover as a record of the moves made.

Techniques

  • Web Audio synthesis for the chords, started by a button press.
  • Canvas2D for the oscillator and membrane views, Chart.js and Plotly for the charts.
  • Tailwind from a CDN for layout in the originals.
  • A skin switch on the Weft page swaps between three versions of the file.

Style

Three looks for one idea. The latest Weft page is near-black with neon accents and a geometric display face. The explorer is warm paper with teal and amber. The poster is navy and amber with large figures.
